## GLP-1 and obesity drugs: oral versions, generics/compounding, pricing, adoption, second-order effects

### Takeaway
2026 is the year GLP-1s went mass-market and cash-pay: two oral pills are approved (Novo's Wegovy pill; Lilly's Foundayo/orforglipron, approved Apr 1, 2026), TrumpRx/MFN deals pushed entry cash prices to ~$149-$350/month, Medicare opened a $50/month bridge (Jul 2026), and roughly 11-18% of US adults report current/recent use. Compounding is being squeezed by FDA, pushing telehealth players (Hims) into branded partnerships. The venture opportunity has shifted from "access to the drug" (commoditizing) to the second-order layer: adherence/persistence, muscle/nutrition, maintenance/off-ramping, employer/payer cost management, and the food/fitness/insurance re-plumbing.

### Cited Findings
**Oral GLP-1s and approvals**
- FDA approved Lilly's orforglipron (brand Foundayo) on April 1, 2026 (some sources say Apr 2); fifth approval under the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot and reportedly the fastest NME approval since 2002; once-daily oral, no refrigeration, no food/water restrictions; indicated BMI ≥30 or ≥27 with comorbidity. A US type 2 diabetes submission has since been added (status as of Oct 7, 2026) — [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/conditions/weight-loss/orforglipron-danuglipron-new-weight-loss-drug); [Remy Research (secondary)](https://remyresearch.com/research/is-orforglipron-approved); [Lilly Q1 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000059478/000005947826000043/q126lillysalesandearningsp.htm)
- Novo's Wegovy pill: TrumpRx monthly price falls from $1,349 list to as low as $149 depending on dose — [White House fact sheet, Nov 2025](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-announces-major-developments-in-bringing-most-favored-nation-pricing-to-american-patients/)

**Second-order effects (food)**
- Cornell study: households cut grocery spend ~5.3% within six months of starting a GLP-1; Circana projects GLP-1 households = 35% of F&B units sold by 2030; biggest drops in savory snacks, sweets, baked goods, sugary drinks — [Food Industry Executive, Mar 2026](https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2026/03/what-glp-1-adoption-actually-means-for-your-sku-rationalization-decisions-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- Drug access is commoditizing: with $149–$350 cash prices, branded DTC via manufacturers and TrumpRx, the "compounded GLP-1 telehealth" arbitrage of 2023-25 is largely closed. New entrants competing on drug price are poorly positioned.
- White space is in the "GLP-1 economy" layer: (a) persistence/adherence and dose-titration/off-ramp programs (maintenance after weight loss), (b) muscle preservation, protein/nutrition and strength programs, (c) employer/payer benefit management for GLP-1 spend with outcomes guarantees, (d) food/CPG reformulation tooling and data, (e) cash-pay pharmacy and care navigation now that cash prices don't count toward deductibles. These are mostly capital-efficient software/services plays.
- Medicare's $50 Bridge (through 2027) and CMS's ACCESS "early cardio-kidney-metabolic" track create a reimbursed channel for tech-enabled obesity/CKM care for seniors — a rare Medicare-funded wedge for startups.
- Insurance: cash-pay outside deductibles and Part D sponsor reluctance on BALANCE suggest payers want to keep GLP-1s off-balance-sheet; startups that help employers/payers manage risk (outcomes-based contracts, stop-loss for GLP-1) have tailwinds.

## AI in healthcare: scribes, AI clinicians/triage, prior auth & RCM, FDA stance, consumer AI health, reimbursement

### Takeaway
Clinical AI is the dominant destination for health venture dollars, but the first wave is consolidating fast: Epic (Feb 2026) and athenahealth (Aug 2026, free) bundled ambient scribing into the EHR, squeezing standalone scribes; OpenEvidence ($12B Jan 2026; reportedly $15B Sept 2026) and Abridge ($5.3B) are the category leaders. OpenAI's ChatGPT Health (Jan 2026, all US users Jul 23, 2026) makes the consumer front door an LLM. Policy is opening reimbursed lanes (CMS ACCESS model from Jul 2026; WISeR AI prior auth in Medicare since Jan 2026, though Congress is moving to defund it). Rock Health stopped tagging startups as "AI-enabled" because the label no longer differentiates.

### Inferences
- Horizontal scribing is now an EHR feature; avoid new generic scribe bets. Durable white space is in specialty-specific, agentic workflows that EHRs won't build well (e.g., specialty-specific coding, inbox/ messaging, care-gap closure, post-visit orders and follow-up, home health, behavioral health, dental/vet, non-Epic ambulatory long tail).
- Payment-side AI (prior auth on both payer and provider sides, denials management, coding, claims appeals) is the clearest ROI wedge with willingness to pay; WISeR proves CMS appetite but political risk (defunding) means private-payer and provider-side tools are safer.
- ACCESS + TEMPO is the most important structural policy for seed/A companies: a Medicare recurring-payment pathway for tech-enabled chronic care without full FDA clearance. Startups in CKM, MSK, behavioral health should design for ACCESS outcomes metrics.
- ChatGPT Health makes "consumer health LLM chat" a commodity front door; white space shifts to things LLMs can't do alone: ordering labs/prescriptions, licensed clinician fulfillment, care navigation with payer data, and record-aggregation infrastructure (FHIR/TEFCA plumbing, consent).
- Capital concentration (45% of H1 dollars in $100M+ rounds) and no IPO window means seed/A investors must underwrite M&A exits or very fast revenue ramps.

## AI for drug discovery and biology: virtual cells, protein design, AI drugs in trials, self-driving labs, China licensing

Capital is massive and concentrated (Xaira $1B+, Lila ~$550M with talks at much higher valuations, Biohub's $1.8B virtual cell consortium Oct 7, 2026). - Capital-efficient white space: picks-and-shovels for the AI-bio stack — perturbation/data generation services, wet-lab-in-the-loop software, evaluation/benchmarking (given Arc's finding that models often fail to beat baselines), lab-automation orchestration software, regulatory/clinical-ops AI (trial design, site selection, CMC documentation), and cross-border licensing diligence tools.
- Mega-rounds (Xaira, Lila, Isomorphic, Biohub consortium) mean foundation-model building in bio is a scale game; early-stage funds should avoid competing on base models.

## Longevity and consumer health: diagnostics, wearables, biomarkers, women's health, mental health, fertility

### Takeaway
Consumer health is back at the center: 46% of US adults own a wearable, Oura filed to IPO at ~$15.6B on ~$1.4B revenue (status unclear — reportedly postponed), Function Health is valued $2.5B and raised $450M revenue-linked capital (Jul 2026), and Neko Health raised $700M at ~$7B (Jul 2026). ## Healthcare system pressures: aging, clinician shortages, Medicaid changes, costs

### Takeaway
The OBBBA (signed Jul 2025) drives the biggest US coverage contraction in a decade: Medicaid work requirements (80 hrs/month) start Jan 1, 2027 under a June 2026 CMS interim final rule, with estimates of 10–15M fewer insured over ten years; 340B reimbursement cuts erode safety-net/rural margins; a $50B Rural Health Transformation Fund ($10B/yr 2026–2030) partially offsets. Workforce shortfalls (~86,000 physicians over the next decade) plus enterprise buyers' preference for consolidated platforms define the pain points: administrative burden, eligibility/enrollment churn, rural access, and labor productivity.

### Cited Findings
- OBBBA Medicaid work requirements: 80 hrs/month for able-bodied expansion adults, effective Jan 1, 2027; CMS interim final rule June 2026, effective Jul 31, 2026; AAMC says rule exceeds statute; AMA filed amicus supporting 26 states challenging narrowed "medical frailty" definition — [AMA, Mar 13, 2026](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/advocacy-update/march-13-2026-national-advocacy-update); [AAMC](https://www.aamc.org/advocacy-policy/washington-highlights/aamc-responds-cms-medicaid-work-requirements-rule); [AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/federal-advocacy/4-big-beautiful-bill-changes-will-reshape-care-2026)
- Coverage impact estimate: 10–15M fewer insured over 10 years — [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/articles/26/obbba-for-providers)
- Rural Health Transformation Fund: $50B, $10B/yr to states 2026–2030, half split equally among approved states — [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/breaking-down-obbba-how-healthcare-providers-can-prepare-for-the-impact-of-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act)
- Physician shortage: projected 86,000 over next decade (cited by AMA; AAMC attribution unconfirmed) — [AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/advocacy-update/march-13-2026-national-advocacy-update)
- Reduced 340B reimbursements further erode safety-net and rural margins — [Healthcare Business Today](https://www.healthcarebusinesstoday.com/obbba-healthcare-funding-shifts-providers/)
- Enterprise buyers consolidating vendors, cutting unintegrated point solutions; late-stage startups pushed to recaps/M&A — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/10/12/rock-health-q3-2026-market-update-fifteen-years-digital-health-venture-funding-deployable-value/)
- Medicare cost pressure on GLP-1s: Part D sponsors balked at BALANCE — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicare/what-to-know-about-the-balance-model-for-glp-1s-in-medicare-and-medicaid/)

### Inferences
- Biggest pain points with clear buyers: (1) Medicaid eligibility verification/work-requirement compliance tech for states, MCOs and FQHCs (hard 2027 deadline — a classic "compliance wave" like ACA 2014); (2) uncompensated-care/RCM tools for safety-net and rural hospitals; (3) clinician-productivity AI for understaffed settings (rural, home health, nursing, behavioral health); (4) rural care delivery funded by state RHT allocations.
- Coverage losses push more patients toward cash-pay — reinforcing the TrumpRx/DTC cash market and consumer navigation tools.
- Aging populations + Medicare reimbursed tech models (ACCESS, GLP-1 Bridge) make Medicare Advantage/senior-focused tech enabled services a durable, if regulated, market.

## Which subareas fit seed/Series A capital efficiency vs. capital-intensive?

### Takeaway
Software/services that sit on top of newly reimbursed or newly mandated workflows (payment/admin AI, ACCESS-model chronic care, Medicaid compliance, GLP-1 adjacent services, AI-bio tooling) are seed/A-efficient. Foundation models for biology, AI-first therapeutics, autonomous labs, imaging hardware/scan centers and wearables hardware now require $100M–$1B+ rounds and are dominated by mega-funded incumbents.

### Inferences
**Seed/A-efficient (candidate 20x+ zones)**
- Payer/provider administrative AI: prior auth, denials/appeals, coding, eligibility — fast ROI, enterprise budgets, AI-native margins; beware EHR bundling.
- Specialty/vertical clinical agents in settings Epic underserves (home health, behavioral, dental, independent practices, post-acute, pharmacy).
- ACCESS-model tech-enabled chronic care (CKM, MSK, behavioral) — Medicare recurring revenue with FDA enforcement discretion.
- Medicaid work-requirement/eligibility compliance infrastructure for states, MCOs, providers (2027 deadline).
- GLP-1 economy services: persistence/maintenance, nutrition/muscle, employer cost management, cash-pay navigation.
- Consumer health "action layer" on top of ChatGPT Health/wearables: data aggregation APIs, clinician fulfillment, longitudinal care.
- AI-bio tooling: evaluation/benchmarks, lab orchestration software, perturbation data-as-a-service, clinical trial ops AI, China-asset diligence/licensing intelligence.

**Capital-intensive (late-stage or avoid at seed)**
- Bio foundation models / virtual cells; AI-first therapeutics pipelines; autonomous wet labs; imaging scan networks (Prenuvo/Neko); new wearable hardware; generic horizontal scribes (now commoditized by EHRs).
