The private, late-stage, NA/EU set has split into three groups. (a) Mega-capitalized AI-drug platforms are priced at $4-20B before any decisive clinical data: Isomorphic, Lila, Periodic, Chai, Xaira. ## Q2: Has any AI-discovered drug produced decisive Phase 2/3 data by 2026? Implications for value capture

### Takeaway
Yes, but with heavy caveats, and the value has accrued mostly to the pharma acquirer, not the AI platform. Takeda's zasocitinib (TAK-279), originated by Nimbus using physics- and computation-heavy design (often labelled "AI-designed" in the press), hit every Phase 3 endpoint, beat BMS's Sotyktu head-to-head, and received an NDA with FDA priority review. Takeda had bought it for $4B upfront. Insilico's rentosertib has a positive but small Phase 2a in IPF and is moving to Phase 3. Recursion has only encouraging Phase 2 signals.

### Cited Findings
- Zasocitinib (TYK2, psoriasis): Phase 3 LATITUDE 3001/3002 met all primary and ranked secondary endpoints (Dec 2025). PASI 75 was 76% and 71% vs about 12% for placebo — [pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/takeda-plans-filings-sotyktu-rival-new-psoriasis-data); [Derm Digest](https://thedermdigest.com/topline-results-takedas-oral-tyk2-inhibitor-performs-well-in-phase-3-pso-trials/).
- Head-to-head vs Sotyktu: complete skin clearance was more than 2.5x Sotyktu's rate at week 16 — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/takedas-ai-designed-pill-bests-bms-sotyktu-in-head-to-head-psoriasis-trial).
- FDA accepted the NDA with priority review; launch is targeted for 2027 — [Takeda newsroom](https://www.takeda.com/newsroom/newsreleases/2026/fda-priority-review-zasocitinib-psoriasis/); [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/takeda-pharmaceutical-showcases-strong-phase-iii-psoriasis-data-for-zasocitinib-eyes-2027-launch-2026-03-28/).
- Takeda bought it from Nimbus for $4B upfront (Dec 2022). The "AI-designed" label comes from press characterization; Takeda's releases don't use it — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/takedas-ai-designed-pill-bests-bms-sotyktu-in-head-to-head-psoriasis-trial).
- Rentosertib (Insilico, TNIK, IPF): Phase 2a in 71 patients, published in Nature Medicine (Jun 2025). The 60 mg arm showed a +98.4 mL FVC change, but only 67% of patients completed high-dose treatment vs 88% on placebo. Insilico is moving to Phase 3. A 2026 Nature Biotechnology exploratory analysis found aging-clock reductions (the CEO is an author) — [Drug Discovery Trends](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/insilicos-ai-designed-rentosertib-shows-promise-in-first-phase-2a-trial-results/); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260910/Rentosertib-shifts-multiple-proteomic-aging-clocks-toward-younger-age-profiles-in-IPF-trial.aspx). (Insilico is HK-listed and HQ'd in HK/Boston, so it is out of scope as a target.)
- Recursion (public): REC-4881 in FAP showed about 43% median polyp burden reduction and a 75% response rate at 4 mg (up to 53% at week 25). More data is due Nov 2026 and in 1H27. Its earlier CCM drug was safe but efficacy was unclear. The ALDER Phase 2 result was not found — [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/recursion-study-results-cerebral-cavernous-malformation-ai-drug/725846/); [Investing.com Q2 2026](https://ca.investing.com/news/company-news/recursion-q2-2026-slides-ai-platform-advances-despite-revenue-miss-93CH-4777631).

### Inferences
- The first "decisive" computationally designed win (zasocitinib) went to a company that owned the asset (Nimbus) and sold it, not to a platform licensing its tools. In this case value went to (1) the asset owner at exit and (2) the big-pharma acquirer with commercial reach. That argues for AI biotechs that own wholly-owned assets and keep late-stage economics (Isomorphic, Xaira and Chai say they plan to) over pure tool or SaaS vendors.
- The AI-designed molecules that worked so far hit well-validated targets (TYK2) with better chemistry. AI has not yet proven it raises the probability of success on novel biology. The 2026 valuations of Isomorphic, Chai and Lila are bets that it will.
- Generate's 2026 IPO and Iambic's reported 2026 IPO suggest public markets are a liquidity path for the mid-tier, which caps upside for late private entrants.

### Gaps
- Exscientia/Recursion, Schrödinger-partnered and Isomorphic clinical milestones were not searched. I found no confirmation that Isomorphic has dosed its first human.
- I did not verify the status of rentosertib's Phase 3 start.

## Q3: Which business models historically let tech-bio reach $50B+ outcomes?

### Takeaway
Based on public precedent (prior knowledge, labelled; little was searched), $50B+ outcomes in biology have come from **owning drugs and commercializing them** (Genentech, Amgen, Gilead, Regeneron, Vertex, Moderna at its peak), not from tools or platform licensing. Platform companies (Schrödinger, Recursion, AbCellera, Illumina in tools) have mostly stayed below $50B. In healthcare services and software, $50B+ outcomes have come from owning the workflow or system of record, or from payer/provider scale (Epic, private; Veeva; UnitedHealth/Optum).

### Cited Findings
- Example of value accruing to the asset owner: Nimbus sold zasocitinib for $4B upfront, and Takeda captures the commercial value — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/takedas-ai-designed-pill-bests-bms-sotyktu-in-head-to-head-psoriasis-trial).
- OpenEvidence monetizes clinician attention through ads, a Doximity-like media model — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/01/21/healthcare-ai-startup-openevidence-raises-250m-12b-valuation/).
- Xaira is shifting toward partnering despite $1.3B raised, a sign that even well-funded platforms mix models — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/business/secretive-ai-drug-hunter-xaira-dips-toe-into-dealmaking-with-hunt-for-partners).

### Inferences
- The historical $50B+ path in biotech has been (a) a platform that generates multiple owned blockbusters (Genentech's recombinant DNA, Regeneron's VelocImmune, Vertex's CF franchise, Moderna's mRNA) plus (b) vertical integration into commercialization. Fees and milestones from partnerships rarely compound to $50B.
- For AI drug discovery, the "who captures value" answer is probably: whoever owns the IP on the molecules and the clinical data loop, plus whoever controls distribution (pharma). The model layer risks commoditization. Open models such as Boltz, the seed-stage a16z-backed open-source effort, show structure prediction commoditizing — [Cure tracker](https://wewillcure.com/insights/news/ai-and-machine-learning/ai-drug-discovery-venture-funding-tracker-2026).
- In healthcare AI, a company can reach $50B+ if it becomes infrastructure: clinical decision layer plus distribution (OpenEvidence), documentation embedded in the EHR (Abridge), or AI labor substituting for clinical staff at scale (Hippocratic). Epic's bundling of ambient scribing is the key threat (prior knowledge, not sourced here).
- BCI: the $50B+ path requires becoming a regulated platform device with reimbursement (the Medtronic/Intuitive Surgical analogy) and, eventually, an elective consumer interface. The latter is speculative.

### Gaps
- No sourced historical analysis (e.g., the market caps of Genentech, Regeneron, Vertex, Moderna) was retrieved within the tool budget. The precedents above come from general knowledge and should be checked by the writer.

- The best risk-adjusted 50x shapes for a Series B to late-stage generalist look like: (1) BCI companies at $1-1.5B with near-term regulated products (Science, Precision, Paradromics if it raises); (2) asset-owning AI-bio before its first clinical readout, at an entry under $4B; (3) Merge Labs if a Series B is priced below about $3B.
