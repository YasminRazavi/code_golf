# Hunt the Next Anthropic Below $5B

In late October 2026, a credible 50x from a growth-stage entry is only possible below about a $5B post-money, and in rare cases up to $10B. It is found in companies that are attacking a physical or biological bottleneck the market has not yet priced. The famous AI names are now out of reach on price. With 25% follow-on dilution, a $5B entry needs a **~$333B exit** to return 50x, and a $20B entry needs **~$1.33T**. Only a handful of venture-backed companies have ever reached that scale (SpaceX, and Anthropic and OpenAI on private marks). On a seven-part weighted rubric, the strongest new picks are as follows. **Science Corp** (BCI, ~$1.25-1.5B) scores highest, followed by **Ayar Labs** (optical I/O, ~$3.8B), **Proxima Fusion** (€2.4B), **Substrate** (X-ray lithography, ~$1B on a stale mark), **NewLimit** (longevity, $3.1B), **MatX** (LLM silicon, est. $3.3-6.3B), **Chai Discovery** ($3.8B), **AMI Labs** (~$4.5B), **Cambridge Aerospace** ($3.4B) and **The Exploration Company** (~$2B+). The single highest-scoring name is already in Spark's portfolio. **Oratomic**, the fault-tolerant quantum company whose Series A Spark co-led, scores 86/100 against the shortlist leader's 84, so the first dollar of new conviction may be best spent on pro-rata there. The market setting favors a disciplined buyer. Q3 2026 growth deal value fell about 40% quarter on quarter. Rates rose in September. Anthropic's IPO, expected to price in November, should recycle liquidity into the next cohort. Many valuations below rest on aggregator reports or "in talks" figures. They are flagged throughout and must be verified before any term sheet.

## The 2026 market priced the obvious winners out of 50x range

The two best-documented growth and venture entries that returned 50x+ share one shape. Founders Fund invested in SpaceX in 2008 at about **$315M post-money**, when the company was fighting for survival. SpaceX priced its IPO in June 2026 at about **$1.77T** and closed its first day near $2.1T ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/founders-fund-spacex-50-billion-windfall); [IPOScoop](https://www.iposcoop.com/?p=38639)). Spark's own Anthropic position, entered at roughly **$4B**, was marked against a **$965B** post-money in Q2 2026. WSJ-derived reports put Spark's stake at about $7B ([KuCoin/WSJ summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/spark-capital-s-75m-anthropic-bet-now-worth-7b); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/09/pitchbook-us-venture-funding-hits-412-7b)).¹ In both cases the entry came at a moment of doubt. Both companies were mission-first, were capital-intensive enough that capital itself became a moat, and had governance built for a ten-year game. Their entry prices were also tiny next to the eventual market. That last condition is the one the 2026 market has erased for the obvious names.

The arithmetic is unforgiving. The required exit equals 50 × entry ÷ (1 − dilution). Late-stage AI companies now raise follow-ons measured in tens of billions; Anthropic raised $65B in one round. A realistic dilution assumption is therefore 25-40% unless Spark defends pro-rata.

| Entry post-money | Exit for 50x, no dilution | 25% dilution | 40% dilution |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1B | $50B | $67B | $83B |
| $2B | $100B | $133B | $167B |
| $5B | $250B | $333B | $417B |
| $10B | $500B | $667B | $833B |
| $20B | $1.0T | $1.33T | $1.67T |

The record **992 US unicorns** are worth a combined **$5.7T**, an average of under $6B each ([SiliconANGLE, Oct 2026](https://siliconangle.com/?p=849755)). Outcomes above $250B remain a handful in venture history. For Spark Growth V, which reports about $1.4B in commitments ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/spark-capital)), a single 50x on a $50M check returns about $2.5B, nearly 1.8x the fund. That is why the rubric below values the reach of the tail outcome over the median outcome.

The market cycle now favors a disciplined buyer. H1 2026 US deal value hit **$412.7B**, nearly 30% more than all of 2025. Rounds of $100M+ took 87.5% of value ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/09/pitchbook-us-venture-funding-hits-412-7b)). Q3 then cooled: deal value fell to **$98.4B, down about 40%** quarter on quarter, mostly from growth rounds. No AI company IPO'd in the quarter. 2021-vintage companies trade at a **median 59% discount** on Forge ([SiliconANGLE, Oct 2026](https://siliconangle.com/?p=849755)). The Fed raised 25bp in September and held at 3.75-4.00% on October 28 with two hawkish dissents ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/fomc-october-2026-fed-holds-rates-in-a-10-2-vote)). Hyperscaler capex for 2026 is about **$720-745B**. On October 29, however, Google and Microsoft held off on raising their forecasts, the first sign of hesitation ([TMT Finance](https://www.tmtfinance.com/intel/2026-hyperscaler-capex-tops-us700bn-analysis); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/microsoft-google-ai-capex-estimates.html)).

Two consequences follow. First, a 2027 digestion phase would hit the most levered and most crowded layers first: neoclouds, inference platforms and application companies at 40-80x ARR. The long-duration physical bets on this shortlist (photonics, fusion, lithography, BCI) would be hit least. Second, Elad Gil's warning that fast enterprise AI revenue "doesn't mean they're going to stick" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/03/elad-gil-on-which-ai-markets-have-winners-and-which-are-still-wide-open)) argues against paying up for application-layer momentum. Coding, customer service and search are the categories frontier labs most want to absorb. SpaceX's $60B purchase of Cursor at about 20x ARR set a ceiling on what even the best coding app fetched ([Wikipedia: Anysphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anysphere)).

## A seven-part rubric weights entry price as heavily as vision

The rubric turns the shared traits of the SpaceX and Anthropic entries into seven criteria. Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5 and weighted to a 100-point total. "Room to 50x" carries the largest weight because it is the one constraint no amount of vision can overcome.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 looks like | What a 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Room to 50x given entry** | 20 | 50x exit needed (no dilution) under $100B | 50x exit needed above $750B |
| **S-curve inflection** | 15 | Decisive catalyst inside the fund's life (product launch, regulatory clearance, first revenue ramp) while the market still doubts | Proof point a decade away, or already fully priced |
| **Epoch-defining scale** | 15 | Attacks a civilizational bottleneck (energy, compute, chips, disease, aging) or a labor pool the size of a wage bill | Category ceiling in the tens of billions |
| **Structural moat** | 15 | Physics, regulation, capital intensity or proprietary physical data that a frontier lab cannot copy with a model release | Feature lead over models that are commoditizing |
| **Mission and judgment-day defense** | 15 | A mission Yasmin could defend to LPs, founders and her own conscience even if the company fails | Mission is commercial only, or ethically contested |
| **Founder and team** | 10 | Category-defining technical founders with a track record | Unproven team, or a key-person gap |
| **Spark fit and conflicts** | 10 | Spark can lead or co-lead; no portfolio or Anthropic conflict; adjacent to Spark's history | Direct conflict with an Anthropic or Spark position, or wrong stage |

The Room score uses explicit brackets on the 50x exit needed before dilution: under $100B scores 5, $100-250B scores 4, $250-400B scores 3, $400-750B scores 2, and above $750B scores 1. When a mark is stale in a sector that re-rated fast in 2026, the score drops one notch, because the next round will almost certainly price higher. MatX and Unconventional AI are marked down for this reason. Etched, for comparison, went from about $5B to $21B in eight months ([WOWTALE](https://en.wowtale.net/2026/08/19/234777/)). All scores are analyst judgment built on the cited facts. They are a structured way to argue, not a measurement.

## Ten picks, each with a failure mode and a defense

### 1. Science Corp: the cheapest entry into the human-computer interface

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** Science Corp, founded by Max Hodak, raised **$230M at a $1.25B post-money**; another source says $1.5B. The proceeds fund the commercial launch of its **PRIMA retinal implant** ([TechBuzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/science-corp-secures-230m-to-fast-track-brain-implant-launch); [Neurofounders](https://www.neurofounders.co/articles/bci-enters-a-new-funding-stage)). BCI is the interface layer that comes after screens and headsets, and Spark has a history in interfaces through Oculus. Neuralink, the category leader, last priced at about $9-9.65B in 2025. Secondary-market estimates in 2026 run to **~$47-59B** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/neuralink/); [Neura Pod](https://www.neurapod.com/neuralink-valuation); [Private Market View](https://privatemarketview.com/companies/neuralink/valuation)). If that is what the market will pay for the leading platform, a $1.25-1.5B entry into the nearest-to-revenue challenger is the cleanest Room-to-50x position on the list.

**What has to be true for 50x.** The company needs to reach roughly **$75-100B** after dilution. PRIMA must win approval and reimbursement as a vision-restoration product, which gives the company revenue, manufacturing scale and regulatory muscle. That platform must then extend to cortical read/write interfaces before Neuralink locks up the category. The Medtronic and Intuitive Surgical path, a regulated platform device with reimbursement, is the precedent for a $50B+ outcome.

**How it fails.** Regulatory timelines slip. Reimbursement stays narrow, and the retinal indication turns out to be a niche. Neuralink or Precision wins the high-bandwidth cortical platform. The valuation conflict ($1.25B vs $1.5B) and the absence of revenue data are diligence gaps.

**Judgment-day defense.** "We funded the effort to give sight back to blind people." That mission survives failure intact. **Paired alternative: Precision Neuroscience** closed a **$250M Series D in Sept 2026**, co-led by Pershing Square. It holds FDA 510(k) clearance for its Layer 7 interface for up to 30 days of implantation, and a single source puts its valuation above $1B ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/precision-neuroscience-raises-250m-brain-computer-interface); [med-tech.world](https://med-tech.world/news/precision-neuroscience-250m-funding/)). It scores identically. Meet both and back the one with the shorter path to a permanent-implant approval.

### 2. Ayar Labs: optical I/O is a physics bottleneck, not a feature race

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** Copper interconnect is running out of room as AI systems scale from racks to rack-to-rack fabrics. Ayar's optical I/O chiplets replace electrical I/O with light. It raised a **$500M Series E in March 2026** (total $870M) at a reported **$3.75-3.8B**, added **$150M around September** including a strategic from Wiwynn, and targets a product ramp in 2028 AI systems ([Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/connect/2026/03/04/ayar-labs-gets-500-million-to-ramp-photonics-into-2028-ai-systems/4093515); [WOWTALE](https://en.wowtale.net/2026/09/15/235110/)). More than **$15B changed hands in silicon photonics** between late 2025 and mid-2026, including Marvell's $3.25B purchase of Celestial AI ([optim.vc](https://www.optim.vc/the-state-of-silicon-photonics-whos-building-whos-buying-and-where-the-industry-is-heading/)). The bottleneck is real, and strategic buyers are paying for it.

**What has to be true for 50x.** Co-packaged optics must become mandatory for scale-up beyond copper, and Ayar must become the merchant standard: the "Broadcom of optical I/O." That implies a ~$190-250B company riding the projected **$1T+ in 2027 hyperscaler capex** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/microsoft-google-ai-capex-estimates.html)).

**How it fails.** Nvidia or Broadcom internalizes the technology, the 2028 ramp slips, or the Celestial precedent repeats and an incumbent acquires the company at a single-digit-billion price. That outcome is a fine return but not a 50x. The valuation is secondary-sourced.

**Judgment-day defense.** Cutting the energy per bit of AI compute lowers the power cost of intelligence itself, which matters whichever lab wins. **Paired alternative: Lightmatter** last priced at **$4.4B in 2024** ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/lightmatter/__UzjACTIII619AffSL1FYPgASKG7oNo0LdOcRvQnFRl8)). That mark is two years stale. If its next round prices flat, it becomes as attractive as Ayar.

### 3. Proxima Fusion: Europe's fusion champion at a sixth of the US price

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** Power is the binding constraint on AI. Proxima raised **€411M at €2.4B** on July 7, 2026, led by XTX Ventures and East X Ventures, with **Google and RWE** as strategics and KfW Capital and SPRIND participating. It calls this the largest European fusion round on record ([Proxima](https://www.proximafusion.com/press-news/proxima-fusion-raises-eu411-million-to-build-europes-commercial-fusion-champion); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/07/google-proxima-fusion-funding.html)). Helion, by comparison, priced at **$15.5B** a month earlier ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/helion-hits-15-5b-valuation-with-465m-in-new-cash-to-commercialize-fusion-this-decade/)). That is the same TAM, which is all of global electricity, at about one-sixth the entry price. Europe also has a sovereignty reason to crown a champion.

**What has to be true for 50x.** The quasi-isodynamic stellarator path must de-risk on its demonstrator. Proxima must keep its European state and strategic backers, and become the continent's fusion utility-supplier. Fusion financing will be dilutive, so the realistic bar is **~€160-200B**.

**How it fails.** The physics or engineering fails. More likely, timelines run past the fund's life, and repeated large rounds dilute early growth investors. This pick scores lowest on S-curve and Spark fit for that reason. Commercial power is a 2030s event.

**Judgment-day defense.** Clean, abundant baseload power is about as defensible a mission as exists. **Paired alternative: Commonwealth Fusion Systems** has raised about $4B in total, including $1B in July 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/19/every-fusion-startup-that-has-raised-over-100m/)). Its valuation is undisclosed (estimated at $5-9B, low confidence). It has the nearer SPARC catalyst but a higher price.

### 4. Substrate: the only credible way around ASML, at a seed-era price

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** Substrate is building **X-ray lithography from a particle-accelerator light source** and plans to run its own fabs rather than sell tools. It raised **$100M at about $1B in October 2025** from Founders Fund, General Catalyst, Valor and Peter Thiel ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/us-startup-substrate-raises-100m-for-development-of-lithography-tools-to-challenge-asml/); [TechInsights](https://www.techinsights.com/blog/chip-insider-substrate-x-ray-lithography)). Leading-edge chipmaking depends on a single toolmaker. A working alternative would be strategic for the US and an ASML/TSMC-scale prize. Coverage of the company cites a possible **90% cost reduction**, which is a company claim ([ic-pcb](https://www.ic-pcb.com/substrate-bets-on-x-ray-lithography-to-challenge-asml-and-slash-chip-production-costs-by-90.html)).

**What has to be true for 50x.** First production wafers and customer qualification must arrive before the end of the decade. At the stale price, 50x needs only about **$50-67B**, the lowest bar on the shortlist. The next round will almost certainly price higher, so the real bar is likely $150B+.

**How it fails.** Serious independent analysts doubt the viability of X-ray lithography ([Vik's Newsletter](https://www.viksnewsletter.com/p/an-in-depth-look-at-the-viability)), and owning fabs is extremely capital-intensive. This is the most technically binary pick on the list.

**Judgment-day defense.** "We tried to remove a single point of failure from the world's chip supply chain." **Alternates in the same slot:** **Fab2** (formerly Atomic Semi, co-founded by Jim Keller and Sam Zeloof) raised a **$500M Series A at $3.7B** in Sept 2026 ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/149546-formerly-atomicsemi-fab2-raises-500m-series-a-at-a-3-7b-valuation-to-mas/)). It scores 75, but the round is below Spark's stage and came after a ~37x step-up. **xLight**, a free-electron-laser EUV source with a $150M CHIPS agreement, is in talks to raise $350M ([aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/xlight-in-talks-to-raise-350m-after-us-chips-act-award)). It carries lower adoption risk because it sells into ASML, but its outcome is also smaller.

### 5. NewLimit: an owned-asset bet that aging is treatable

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** NewLimit raised a **$435M Series C at $3.1B in June 2026**, led by Founders Fund. Eli Lilly participated in its previous round at $1.6B, and its first program targets the aging liver ([Quartz](https://qz.com/newlimit-series-c-fundraise-aging-liver-trial-060326); [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/venture-capital/newlimit-series-c-longevity-biotech-founders-fund)). If epigenetic reprogramming works, the addressable market is every person who ages.

**What has to be true for 50x.** The bar is **$155-207B**. NewLimit must own its molecules and keep late-stage economics. Recent evidence shows value accrues to asset owners, not to tool vendors. Takeda paid Nimbus **$4B upfront** for zasocitinib, which then beat BMS's Sotyktu head-to-head in Phase 3 ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/takedas-ai-designed-pill-bests-bms-sotyktu-in-head-to-head-psoriasis-trial)). Public markets are open to bio: **12 of the 18** venture-backed IPOs in Q3 2026 were healthcare ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/?p=849755)).

**How it fails.** The biology does not translate, the first program yields a niche indication rather than a platform, or safety findings stall the field. Total capital raised is reported inconsistently ($347M vs $682M; [Contrary](https://research.contrary.com/company/newlimit)).

**Judgment-day defense.** Few missions are easier to defend than trying to extend healthy human life.

### 6. MatX: the cheapest seat at the LLM-silicon table, if Spark is not already in it

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** MatX was founded by ex-Google TPU engineers. It raised a **$500M Series B in February 2026**, led by **Jane Street and Situational Awareness** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/24/nvidia-challenger-ai-chip-startup-matx-raised-500m/)). The valuation was undisclosed; databases estimate **~$4.65B, within a $3.3-6.3B range** ([newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-chip-top-startups-valuation)). Its closest comparable, Etched, went from about $5B to **$21B** in eight months and has reportedly drawn bids at **$40-50B** ([American Bazaar](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/10/06/etched-draws-funding-offers-at-over-40-billion-valuation-489501/)). Cerebras's May 2026 IPO raised about $5.55B ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/cerebras-launches-ipo-20260515)), which confirms there is an exit market for AI silicon.

**What has to be true for 50x.** MatX must become the #2 merchant accelerator behind Nvidia, or a lab or hyperscaler standard on the model of the TPU, in a **$230-310B** outcome.

**How it fails.** Nvidia's roadmap closes the gap, an architecture bet is overtaken by model changes, or tape-out slips. The Room score is cut to 3 because the February mark is stale while the sector re-rates.

**Judgment-day defense.** Cheaper compute broadens access to intelligence and weakens a single-vendor chokepoint. **Conflict flag:** one aggregator lists MatX among Spark's 2026 participations ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/investors/spark-capital)). Confirm this internally first. If Spark is in, MatX becomes a follow-on decision, not a new pick.

### 7. Chai Discovery: AI drug design, but only if it owns the molecules

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** Chai raised a **$400M Series C at $3.8B in July 2026**, led by Index with Sequoia, Dimension and Kleiner Perkins participating. The valuation roughly tripled from $1.3B in seven months ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/chai-discovery-400m-series-c-ai-drug-discovery); [Cure tracker](https://wewillcure.com/insights/news/ai-and-machine-learning/ai-drug-discovery-venture-funding-tracker-2026)). If foundation models really raise the probability of success on new biology, the first company to prove it owns the next Genentech.

**What has to be true for 50x.** Chai must take owned assets into the clinic and keep the economics, rather than live on partnership fees, which rarely compound to $50B+. The bar is about **$190-253B**.

**How it fails.** Model-layer commoditization is the core risk. Open structure-prediction efforts such as Boltz (an a16z-backed seed) are already shipping ([Cure tracker](https://wewillcure.com/insights/news/ai-and-machine-learning/ai-drug-discovery-venture-funding-tracker-2026)). AI's proven wins so far have been better chemistry on known targets, not new biology. Isomorphic, backed by Alphabet, has raised $2.1B in a single round.

**Judgment-day defense.** Compressing a decade of drug discovery is a mission that holds even in failure. Xaira, with an unverified ~$2.7-3.5B valuation, is the alternate.

### 8. AMI Labs: a hedge against the transformer itself

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** AMI Labs is in Paris, with Yann LeCun as executive chair and Alexandre LeBrun as CEO. It raised **$1.03B at a $3.5B pre-money (~$4.5B post)** in March 2026 from Nvidia, Samsung, Temasek and Toyota Ventures, to build non-LLM world models ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/yann-lecuns-ami-labs-raises-1-03-billion-to-build-world-models/); [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2026/03/shorooq-invests-ami-labs-ai-startup-hits-3-5-billion-valuation)). It is the purest "different paradigm" option in Europe. Structurally, it is the closest thing on the list to an early research lab with a founding scientist, as Anthropic was in 2023.

**What has to be true for 50x.** World models must beat scaled LLMs in physical reasoning and planning, which would make AMI a foundational layer for robotics and embodied AI. The bar is about **$225-300B**.

**How it fails.** The research path is long, and the team was reportedly about a dozen people as of mid-2026 ([FutureSearch](https://futuresearch.ai/ami-labs-forecast/)). Transformers may simply keep absorbing the capabilities world models promise.

**Judgment-day defense.** "We funded open scientific pluralism in machine intelligence, from Europe." **Two cautions** reduce its Mission and Fit scores. If AMI succeeds, it becomes an Anthropic competitor. Diligence should also test how well its safety posture aligns with the thesis behind Spark's Anthropic investment.

### 9. Cambridge Aerospace: cost-per-intercept is the defining defense problem

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** Cheap drones have upended the economics of air defense. Cambridge Aerospace builds low-cost interceptors (Skyhammer). It raised a **$300M Series C at $3.4B in August 2026**, led by DFJ Growth, up from $1.3B in April ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/cambridge-aerospace-raises-300m-at-3-4b-valuation-to-scale-low-cost-air-defence/)). NATO allies have committed to **5% of GDP by 2035** ([NATO](https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/defence-expenditures-and-natos-5-commitment)). Demand is immediate, which is why it scores 5 on S-curve.

**What has to be true for 50x.** It must become the volume supplier of interceptors to European and allied militaries, a **$170-227B** outcome that would require it to out-scale primes such as MBDA and Raytheon.

**How it fails.** Prime contractors respond with their own low-cost lines, procurement cycles stall, or momentum pricing (2.6x in four months) leaves no room for error. Team depth was not researched.

**Judgment-day defense.** The product protects people from attack and is defensive by design. Of the defense names that clear the price bar, it carries the lightest values flag. It is still a weapons company, and that should be stated plainly to LPs.

### 10. The Exploration Company: Europe's ticket to orbit and back

**Why it could be epoch-defining.** The Exploration Company is developing Nyx, the only European crew and cargo capsule. In September 2026 ESA awarded it **€760M under ALADDIN**: €310M for a demo mission that must dock with the ISS by Q2 2029, plus €450M in options ([startuprad.io](https://www.startuprad.io/post/startup-news-germany-austria-switzerland-september-2026)). **What has to be true for 50x.** It must become Europe's sovereign Dragon and then a commercial station-logistics provider. The bar is about **$100-133B**.

**How it fails.** It misses the 2029 docking deadline, development costs overrun, or SpaceX pricing undercuts the commercial business case.

**Judgment-day defense.** Independent European access to space is a strategic public good. **Fit note:** it complements Stoke rather than competing with it, since capsules need launch. It beat **Varda** (score 73, $1.6B, reentry platform) on the tie-break because ESA's anchor contract gives more revenue visibility. **Periodic Labs** (73) is the most Anthropic-shaped name at the top of the price band. Its ex-OpenAI/DeepMind team is building autonomous science labs, with a reported $7.5B round in talks that is not confirmed closed ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/iainmartin/2026/05/07/former-openai-researcher-to-raise-500-million-for-ai-science-startup/)).

## Conflicts, follow-ons and values flags shape the final order

**Spark positions are follow-on decisions, not new picks.** **Oratomic** is the standout. It raised a $300M Series A on July 7, 2026, **co-led by ARCH, Spark and Khosla**, with Bezos Expeditions, Index and General Catalyst participating, to build a ~20,000-qubit fault-tolerant machine ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/oratomic-raises-300m-for-development-of-20000-qubit-fault-tolerant-quantum-computer/)). Humanoids raise milder flags: in-home data privacy (1X NEO teleoperation), labor displacement, and teleoperator data collection in low-wage geographies.

## Conclusion

The "next Anthropic" in 2026 will probably not be an AI lab. The labs and their best-known application companies are priced for success. The fundable 50x shapes are now the hard bottlenecks that AI's demand curve runs into: photons moving between chips, the lithography that makes them, the power that runs them, and, at the human end, the cells that age and the neurons a computer could talk to. These are the places where markets still doubt, capital intensity becomes a moat, and a failed mission remains honorable. That is the profile Spark backed before. The most counterintuitive finding is that the highest-scoring opportunity, Oratomic, is already in the portfolio. Spark's next fund-defining return may depend less on finding a new name than on concentrating hard behind an existing one before its next round reprices it.
