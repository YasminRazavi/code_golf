# Bottlenecks and boring rails beat frontier hype

Yasmin Razavi's best 20x bets for late 2026 sit at the chokepoints the AI buildout keeps running into, and in the unglamorous financial and administrative plumbing that AI is now rewiring. At the top of the list are speed-to-power software and grid hardware for data centers, AI-native "labor-equivalent" services for back-office work, stablecoin payout and treasury rails for business, and trust and payment infrastructure for AI agents. A second tier covers healthcare revenue-cycle agents, AI-for-science tooling, defense autonomy and supply chain, and the inference control plane. The rest of the 15 theses are lower conviction or need an earlier entry than a growth investor usually makes. The math sets the bar. Razavi invests after product-market fit, so an entry at $300M–$2B post-money needs an exit of roughly **$9B–$57B** to return 20x after typical later dilution. That rules out most of the crowded 2026 categories, where the leaders already sit at $10B–$100B. It also rules in a narrower set where bottlenecks are real and dated, buyers are already paying, and her record (Anthropic, Crusoe, MatX, Deel, Kalshi, Edison Scientific, Superblocks) gives her an edge in sourcing and judgment. The main risk to the whole list is macro. An AI-capex pause, a Fed that keeps hiking, or frontier labs moving up the stack would hit most of these theses together. That correlation matters more than any single company's execution risk.

Three facts about the 2026 market shape where these outcomes can come from. First, capital is extremely concentrated. Many of the obvious categories are therefore already priced past the point where 20x is plausible. Second, exits favor infrastructure. Of the 2025 IPO class, "only 3 of 13 are above water" ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-2025-ipo-class-graded-only-3-of-13-are-above-water/)). Strategic M&A for picks-and-shovels layers has been large: **Stripe bought OpenRouter for $7.5B, Nvidia bid $12.9B for Hugging Face, and AMD moved to buy World Labs for $8.2B** ([FourWeekMBA/Crunchbase](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-crunchbase-q3-2026-159b-venture-27-billion-dollar-rounds-ai/)). Third, valuation multiples differ sharply by layer. **My inference from cited figures:** labs trade at roughly 15–21x run-rate (Anthropic at $965B on ~$65B, per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/17/anthropic-says-annualized-revenue-climbed-to-65-billion-in-july.html) and [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/anthropic/)), while top AI apps trade at 25–50x. Those multiples will compress before most growth entries exit, so each thesis below is sized on revenue, not on today's multiples. As a rule of thumb (inference), a $15–30B exit at a mature 10–15x revenue multiple needs **$1–3B of revenue**. That is the bar each end-state below has to clear.

One implication follows directly. New money into the labs cannot 20x from here: Anthropic at $965B would need to be worth about $19T. Razavi's AI-infrastructure edge now pays off around the labs, not in them.

### 1. Speed-to-power software and data-center power orchestration

**Why now.** The binding constraint on AI is power delivery, not chips. LBNL counts about **2,061 GW** waiting in US interconnection queues, and only about **13%** of capacity requested in 2000–2020 ever reached commercial operation ([LBNL Queued Up 2026](https://emp.lbl.gov/publications/queued-2026-edition-characteristics)). ERCOT's large-load queue passed **233 GW against about 7.5 GW actually connected** ([Tech Fund](https://www.techinvestments.io/p/power-bottlenecks-and-the-ai-data)). North American data-center vacancy is at a record-low **1.6%**, and interconnection takes 5–7 years in hot markets ([FolioTrail](https://www.foliotrail.com/articles/state-of-ai-2026-part-1)). An estimated 30–50% of planned 2026 capacity could slip to 2028 or later ([Accuris](https://accuristech.com/blog/ai-data-center-electronic-component-supply/)). Behind-the-meter gas has become the bridge: gas-generator interconnection requests are reportedly up about 160% year over year, and the leading turbine OEM is sold out through 2030 ([Core Insights Review](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/08/data-center-power-infrastructure.html)). Gartner expects data-center power demand to grow about 26–27% in 2026 to about 132 GW, reaching about 290 GW by 2030 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-06-10-gartner-says-data-center-electricity-demand-to-grow-26-percent-in-2026)).

**The 20x path.** The target is a company that sells "time to power": interconnection-study and queue automation, flexible or curtailable load agreements, and orchestration of microgrids that combine on-site gas, storage and grid supply. Hyperscaler capex is about **$725B in 2026**, heading toward roughly **$1.1T in 2027** ([buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/hyperscaler-ai-capex-spending-cloud-infrastructure-2026); [SEC 8-K citing Morgan Stanley](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001591956/000121390026099458/ea030536701ex99-1.htm)). **Inference:** if a platform earns software, services and energy-management fees equal to 0.2–0.3% of annual AI capex, revenue reaches $1.5–3B, which supports a $15–30B outcome. A month of delay on a gigawatt campus is worth far more than the software fee, so pricing power should hold even if capex growth slows.

**Why Razavi.** Her Crusoe board work gives her direct knowledge of how operators buy power, and possibly first look at operators' vendors. Crusoe raised more than $3B in Q3 2026 ([FourWeekMBA/Crunchbase](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-crunchbase-q3-2026-159b-venture-27-billion-dollar-rounds-ai/)). The category also matches her "unsexy workflow with huge enterprise value" pattern, applied to utilities and grid operators.

**Risks and kill criteria.** Drop the thesis if hyperscalers cut 2027 capex guidance materially. Drop a company if its revenue depends on a handful of projects that slip. Watch whether FERC or ERCOT large-load rules standardize flexible interconnection in a way that makes the software a free utility feature. Some analysts argue utility forecasts overstate data-center load by up to 40% ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/datacenterbytes/2026/01/2026-us-data-centers-and-energy-key-trends-shaping-power-demand)), and a 5–10x gap between requests and real projects ([Nexans](https://www.nexans.com/perspective/why-clearing-the-grid-queue-wont-fix-data-centers/)) means demand signals are noisy.

**Signals in a growth-stage company.** Look for contracted megawatts energized, not megawatts "in pipeline." Look for repeat purchases from at least two hyperscalers or neoclouds, measured months saved per project, and gross margins that hold up even when the company delivers hardware.

### 2. AI-native back-office services sold as labor equivalents

**Why now.** Sequoia's "Services: The New Software" argues that **$6 is spent on services for every $1 on software**, and that the next trillion-dollar company will be "a software company masquerading as a services firm" ([theaiopportunities](https://www.theaiopportunities.com/p/sequoia-ai-ascent-2026-the-future)). Menlo found startups capture **91% of AI spend in finance and operations**. It also found that 47% of AI deals reach production versus 25% for SaaS, and that 76% of AI solutions are bought rather than built ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)). METR estimates agent task horizons are doubling about every **4.3 months** ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)), and frontier models complete work at their 50% horizon for about **3% of human cost** ([Redwood Research](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/ais-capability-improvements-havent)). The labor market already shows the substitution: employment of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations is about **19% below trend** ([Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)). Elad Gil describes the business model shift as selling "human labor equivalents" rather than seats ([ceointerviews.ai](https://ceointerviews.ai/quote/ai-is-probably-the-perfect-example-of-this-MzA1NTU/)).

**The 20x path.** The targets are accounting close and reconciliation, tax compliance, payroll exceptions, insurance operations and regulatory filings, priced per outcome against labor budgets. Reported comparables show the scale: Harvey at about $300M ARR with valuations reported at $11B–$15.5B ([wellows](https://wellows.com/blog/ai-startups/)), Sierra at $15.8B ([wellows](https://wellows.com/blog/ai-startups/)), and Decagon tripling to about $4.5B in under six months ([radarkit](https://radarkit.ai/blog/100-hottest-ai-startups-to-watch/)). **Inference:** entering a finance-ops services leader at $400–600M post needs a roughly $12–17B exit, which means about $1B+ of revenue at a mature multiple. That is plausible because the addressable budget is outsourced labor, not software seats.

**Why Razavi.** This is her stated thesis that payroll, tax compliance and reconciliation hold large enterprise value. That framing comes from a secondary compilation, so treat it as reported ([Antoine Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-yasmin-razavi/)). Her Deel experience gives her a playbook for selling compliance-heavy workflows to CFOs, and the Superblocks extension shows she will fund an AI pivot inside an existing enterprise motion.

**Risks and kill criteria.** The main risk is the labs. Anthropic's Claude Cowork launch was blamed for a **$285B software selloff on Feb 3, 2026** ([LongAngle](https://www.longangle.com/alts-education/software-ai)). Gross margins are a second risk: AI products average about 53% versus 77% for SaaS ([LongAngle](https://www.longangle.com/alts-education/software-ai)). Kill a company if a general-purpose agent can replicate its core workflow without proprietary integrations, liability acceptance or regulated licenses, or if gross margin is not rising toward 65%+ as model prices fall.

**Signals.** Look for outcome-based pricing with net revenue retention above 130%, revenue per FTE replaced, the company accepting liability for errors (signing the return, guaranteeing the close), deep system-of-record integrations, and falling inference cost per task. Spark's Anthropic board seat is a conflict to manage, but also a vantage point on what the labs will and will not build.

### 3. Stablecoin B2B payout, FX and treasury orchestration

**Why now.** Stablecoin supply stalled at roughly **$300–320B** and shrank mid-2026 for the first time in four years ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/digital-assets/2026/07/27/the-stablecoin-market-shrank-for-the-first-time-in-four-years-watch-the-volumes-instead/)). Usage kept compounding, though: USDC on-chain volume reached **$14.8T in Q2 2026, up 151%**, while Circle's revenue grew only **7%** as falling rates squeezed reserve income ([Circle](https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-reports-second-quarter-2026-results)). Value is moving from issuance to movement. Stripe and Paradigm's Tempo went live on Mar 18, 2026, with Visa as an anchor validator ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/stripe-and-paradigms-tempo-mainnet-goes-live-for-machine-payments/); [Major Matters](https://majormatters.co/p/visa-stripe-tempo-blockchain-stablecoin)). One source reports Tempo settles Stripe payouts, MoneyGram and **Deel** ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/02/tempo-payment-first-l1-stripe-institutional-blockchain-settlement)), but another guide conflicts on which chains carry Stripe's stablecoin charges ([Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15083174-stripe-stablecoin-payments-2026)). Visa's stablecoin settlement reached a **$7B run rate, up 50% quarter over quarter** ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/04/29/visa-expands-stablecoin-settlement-network-as-volume-hits-usd7-billion-run-rate)). The GENIUS Act bars issuers from paying yield and the OCC proposal presumes affiliate yield workarounds are prohibited ([OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-3.html); [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/genius-act-stablecoin-law/)), which further caps issuer economics.

**The 20x path.** The opportunity is the orchestration layer: multi-rail payouts, FX, treasury, compliance and reconciliation for businesses paying suppliers, contractors and subsidiaries across emerging markets. **Inference:** a 5–10bp take on $1–2T of annual B2B flows yields $0.5–2B of revenue. Endpoints like Revolut (reported ~$115B, [Mobibank](https://www.mobibank.fi/neobank-valuations-2026/)) and the Stripe/OpenRouter-style strategic bid show that $10–20B outcomes are reachable for a rail that owns a corridor or a segment.

**Why Razavi.** This is Deel's adjacent territory. Deel is reportedly an early Tempo user, so she has first-hand insight into what global employers need from payout rails. It also matches the fintech-infrastructure lineage of Marqeta and Capitolis.

**Risks and kill criteria.** Watch whether the CLARITY Act's failure, a 49–50 Senate vote on Sep 15, 2026 ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/senate-fails-to-advance-the-clarity-act)), spills over into hostile treatment of stablecoin payment providers. Watch for Stripe/Tempo or Visa bundling orchestration for free. Kill any company whose revenue is mostly float income or token activity rather than payment fees.

**Signals.** Payment volume that is real B2B (invoices, payroll, supplier settlement) rather than exchange flows, take rates holding as volume grows, licenses in key corridors, and a growing share of volume that never off-ramps to fiat.

### 4. Agent trust, authorization and programmable-spend infrastructure

**Why now.** Agentic commerce has produced too many protocols and too little trust. OpenAI and Stripe's ACP, Google's AP2 (now at FIDO), Coinbase's x402 (now at the Linux Foundation), Stripe/Tempo's MPP and Google's UCP all launched between late 2025 and 2026, and they **are not interoperable today** ([Crossmint](https://www.crossmint.com/learn/agentic-payments-protocols-compared); [Pondero](https://pondero.ai/agents/guides/x402-vs-acp-vs-ap2-agent-payment-protocols-october-2026/)). OpenAI scaled back Instant Checkout in March 2026 after Walmart reportedly saw in-chat checkout convert **about 3x worse** than click-through ([Stellagent](https://stellagent.ai/insights/chatgpt-shopping-feature-deep-dive)). Early x402 volume was inflated by wash trading ([Pondero](https://pondero.ai/agents/guides/x402-vs-acp-vs-ap2-agent-payment-protocols-october-2026/)). a16z frames identity as shifting from KYC to "KYA," meaning cryptographically signed credentials that bind agents to their principals and constraints ([a16z](https://a16z.com/newsletter/big-ideas-2026-part-1/)). Hallucination rates of 22–94% across 26 models ([Stanford AI Index summary](https://dcthemedian.substack.com/p/20-takeaways-from-stanfords-2026)) explain why enterprises will not grant agents spending authority without controls.

**The 20x path.** The target is neutral middleware: agent identity, mandate verification, spend limits, dispute and chargeback handling, and cross-protocol routing. In effect this is card-issuing-style programmable controls for software buyers instead of human ones. **Inference:** if agents mediate even a low-single-digit share of B2B and consumer payments by 2030, the control layer becomes a network-scale business, and the natural acquirers (Visa, Mastercard, Stripe) have shown they pay $7.5B+ for infrastructure. This is the one top-tier thesis where the market is too early for a classic growth entry. A 20x most likely requires Spark's earlier-stage capital at Series A–B (sub-$300M post), or a growth check into a payments company already scaling this as a second product.

**Why Razavi.** It combines her two franchises: frontier-model judgment from Anthropic and issuing and payments-infrastructure judgment from Marqeta. Few investors can underwrite both how agents will behave and how payment controls work.

**Risks and kill criteria.** Card networks or Stripe could make the standard proprietary, or one protocol could win outright and bundle identity in. Consumer distrust could keep agent checkout marginal for years. Kill a company if its volume is mostly self-dealing crypto micropayments, or if it cannot show enterprise customers granting agents real spending authority.

**Signals.** Enterprise policies under management, authorized agent spend, dispute rates on agent-initiated transactions compared with human ones, and integrations with more than one protocol family.

### 5. Healthcare revenue-cycle, prior-auth and eligibility agents

**Why now.** The first wave of clinical AI is being absorbed into EHRs: Epic launched native AI Charting in February 2026, and athenahealth made its scribe free in August ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2026/02/ambient-scribe-ai-startups-epic/); [Plaud](https://www.plaud.ai/blogs/articles/epic-ai-scribe)). Payment-side AI is a different story. CMS's **WISeR** model has run AI-assisted prior authorization in traditional Medicare across six states since Jan 1, 2026 ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/cms-wiser-model)). OBBBA Medicaid work requirements start **Jan 1, 2027**, with an estimated 10–15M fewer insured over ten years ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/federal-advocacy/4-big-beautiful-bill-changes-will-reshape-care-2026); [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/articles/26/obbba-for-providers)). The US faces a projected shortage of **86,000 physicians** ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/advocacy-update/march-13-2026-national-advocacy-update)). Rock Health has stopped tagging startups as "AI-enabled" because the label no longer differentiates, and says buyers now prize workflow ownership, EHR integration and "willingness to absorb liability" ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/10/12/rock-health-q3-2026-market-update-fifteen-years-digital-health-venture-funding-deployable-value/)).

**The 20x path.** The target is an agentic platform covering prior auth, denials and appeals, coding and eligibility for providers, with Medicaid work-requirement verification for states, MCOs and FQHCs as a 2027 compliance wedge. Comparables show capital efficiency: OpenEvidence reportedly reached about $300M annualized revenue on about $700M raised ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/openevidence/)). **Inference:** a $500M entry needs a $14B+ outcome. That likely means an IPO, because health M&A rarely clears $10B, and there has been **no digital-health IPO since HeartFlow in August 2025** ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/10/12/rock-health-q3-2026-market-update-fifteen-years-digital-health-venture-funding-deployable-value/)). Underwrite revenue of $1B+ from cross-selling across the revenue cycle, not a single-module product.

**Why Razavi.** This is an unsexy, compliance-driven workflow with a hard deadline, sold to enterprise buyers with a long GTM cycle. That is the same shape as the Deel trade.

**Risks and kill criteria.** House appropriators voted to defund WISeR ([ASC News](https://ascnews.com/2026/06/a-wiser-move-house-panel-aims-to-halt-funding-for-ai-prior-authorization-model/)), so avoid companies that depend on CMS programs. Epic could extend its bundling into revenue cycle. Kill a company if its win rates fall against EHR-native modules.

**Signals.** Dollars recovered per provider, denial overturn rates, multi-module attach, payer-side as well as provider-side customers, and non-Epic ambulatory long-tail penetration.

### 6. AI-for-science tooling and discovery platforms

**Why now.** AI-designed drugs are reaching late-stage trials. Insilico dosed the first patient in a **Phase III for a generative-AI drug** in September 2026 ([Insilico](https://insilico.com/news/isn1009261-insilico-medicine-doses-first-patient-genesis-ipf-3)), although no AI-designed drug has yet been approved ([AIM Media House](https://aimmediahouse.com/ai-lifesciences/2026-is-the-year-ai-drug-discovery-meets-clinical-reality)). On Oct 7, 2026, a Chan Zuckerberg Biohub-led **$1.8B virtual cell consortium** brought in DeepMind, Meta, Isomorphic, DOE and NIH ([Endpoints](https://endpoints.news/zuckerbergs-biohub-expands-virtual-cell-effort-to-1-8b-with-meta-google/)). Arc Institute's 2025 challenge found that virtual-cell models did **not consistently beat naive baselines** ([Arc Institute](https://arcinstitute.org/news/virtual-cell-challenge-2026)). That is a measurement gap a company can monetize. China now supplies about a third of global out-licensing value, with **$135.7B** in deals in 2025 ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3348295/china-biotech-deals-hit-record-innovative-drugs-draw-interest-multinationals)), which pushes Western pharma to buy speed.

**The 20x path.** The targets are platforms that sell discovery capacity rather than own molecules: closed-loop wet-lab orchestration, perturbation data generation, model evaluation, and clinical-ops AI. Lila Sciences was valued at more than $1.3B after its Series A, with unconfirmed talks near $8.5B ([Yahoo/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/exclusive-ai-lab-lila-sciences-101645391.html); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/lila-sciences-is-testing-how-much-investors-will-pay-for-automated-labs/)), and Periodic Labs reportedly discussed a $7.5B valuation after a $300M seed ([note.com](https://note.com/all_zero/n/n3560a02564fd?hl=en)). Those prices show that a 20x from a $1B+ entry would require a $28B+ outcome. That is credible only for a platform that becomes pharma's outsourced R&D engine. **Inference:** entry needs to be below $700M, before the mega-round step-up.

**Why Razavi.** Edison Scientific is her public bet on this exact thesis, and she has said it ranks among AI's most profound uses.

**Risks and kill criteria.** Bio is a long-duration asset in a hiking-rate environment. Mega-funded players (Xaira $1B+, Isomorphic) could vertically integrate. Kill a company if its models cannot show external benchmark wins, or if revenue is all grants and partnerships with no repeat pharma spend.

**Signals.** Paid pharma programs that renew and expand, wet-lab throughput per dollar, independent benchmark results, and milestone economics on partnered assets.

### 7. Defense autonomy software and non-Chinese drone supply chain

**Why now.** Defense tech VC hit a record **$19.8–24.4B in Q1 2026** and $16.4B in Q2. Autonomous systems took 42.1% of Q2 funding, and PitchBook calls it a **"barbell market"** ([PitchBook Q2](https://pitchbook.com/news/reports/q2-2026-defense-tech-report-go-big-or-go-early-in-the-sectors-barbell-market)). The Pentagon is using Shield AI's Hivemind for low-cost drone and missile programs, driven by the Iran war ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/pentagon-taps-shield-ai-for-low-cost-missile-program.html)). NATO's 5%-of-GDP target and a €150B EU fund steer capital into the supply chain ([WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/gb/insights/blog/the-key-takeaways-from-the-nato-summit/pdf)). On June 22, 2026, China blacklisted MP Materials and USA Rare Earth ([Benchmark Minerals](https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/china-targets-us-rare-earth-and-defence-linked-entities-with-dual-use-export-ban)), which makes Chinese-sourced drone motors and magnets a procurement liability.

**The 20x path.** The targets are mission-autonomy, EW-resilient navigation and C2 software sold into primes and the new integrators, plus attritable-drone components (motors, radios, seekers) with clean supply chains. **Inference:** the "hollowed-out middle" of the barbell is precisely where a growth investor enters. Companies past PMF but below $1B may price at a discount to the $9–13B leaders (Saronic, Shield AI). A $400M entry growing into a Shield AI-scale company returns about 20x after dilution.

**Why Razavi.** She has backed capital-intensive deep tech early (MatX, and Cambridge Aerospace per her Spark bio). She also prefers highly technical founders, which describes the autonomy talent pool.

**Risks and kill criteria.** Procurement lumpiness. The concentration of mega-round capital in Anduril (valued at $61B, in reported talks near $100B per [Defense News/Reuters](https://www.defensenews.com/industry/techwatch/2026/07/24/anduril-in-talks-to-raise-funding-at-about-100-billion-valuation/)), which can build in-house. An Iran ceasefire cooling urgency. Kill a company whose revenue is mostly OTAs and pilots with no program of record within 24 months.

**Signals.** Programs of record, recurring software licenses per platform, production-rate contracts for components, and European as well as US customers.

### 8. Grid hardware bottleneck breakers

**Why now.** Power transformers take about **128 weeks**, and switchgear is described as "sold out through 2028" ([ChargedUp Pro](https://chargeduppro.com/post/data-center-transformer-shortage-power-bottleneck-industrial-property-2026); [Core Insights Review](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/08/data-center-power-infrastructure.html)). OBBBA killed wind and solar credits for projects not under construction by July 4, 2026, but kept storage and firm-power credits into the 2030s. It also extended FEOC "material assistance" tests that reward non-Chinese supply chains ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-impact-on-the-iras-clean-energy-tax-credits.html)). AI compute demand is growing about 4–5x a year against about 35–40% chip-efficiency gains ([FolioTrail](https://www.foliotrail.com/articles/state-of-ai-2026-part-1)), so power density keeps climbing.

**The 20x path.** The targets are solid-state transformers, power-electronics modules and refurbishment or alternative designs that ship in months rather than years. Domestic content earns a pricing premium. **Inference:** a manufacturer reaching $1–2B of revenue at industrial-tech multiples supports a $10–20B outcome, so a 20x needs entry at or below $500M. These businesses need project and working-capital debt, which is harder with venture debt costs rising in a hiking cycle.

**Why Razavi.** Her Crusoe and MatX work shows she will fund hardware when the bottleneck is clear and the customer is a hyperscaler.

**Risks and kill criteria.** Incumbent OEMs expanding capacity, reliability failures in the field, and AI-capex retrenchment. Kill a company that has no utility or hyperscaler qualification after 18 months.

**Signals.** Qualified units in the field, backlog with deposits, gross margin above 30% at scale, and a manufacturing plan funded by non-dilutive or government capital.

### 9. Enterprise inference control plane

**Why now.** The token market has split. Open-weight models, mostly Chinese, carry about **69% of OpenRouter tokens** ([Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/the-thriving-ecosystem-of-open-models/)), while open source was just **11% of enterprise API usage**, with Anthropic at 40% of enterprise share ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)). Price per unit of performance reportedly falls about 50x a year ([VoxBooster, citing Epoch](https://voxbooster.com/blog/ai-inference-cost-statistics-2026/); unverified). Strategic buyers are paying for this layer: Stripe paid **$7.5B for OpenRouter** and Nvidia bid **$12.9B for Hugging Face** ([FourWeekMBA/Crunchbase](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-crunchbase-q3-2026-159b-venture-27-billion-dollar-rounds-ai/)).

**The 20x path.** The target is the enterprise layer that routes across closed and open models, enforces data residency and compliance (including exclusion of Chinese-origin models where required), meters usage-based internal billing, and handles evals. **Inference:** this is a strategic-acquisition outcome in the $8–15B range, so a 20x requires entry at or below $500M.

**Why Razavi.** Her Anthropic seat gives her a view of enterprise model buying, and Superblocks shows her interest in enterprise developer platforms. The Anthropic tie is also a conflict: a router that commoditizes closed models works against her largest position. That has to be managed openly.

**Risks and kill criteria.** This is the most crowded thesis on the list. Baseten raised a $1B+ round ([SiliconANGLE/PitchBook](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/09/pitchbook-us-venture-funding-hits-412-7b-first-half-ai-deals-dominate/)), and Fireworks, Together and Modal compete. The labs and clouds could also bundle routing. Kill any company that is mostly reselling GPU capacity.

**Signals.** Governed spend under management, gross margin on the software layer separate from compute pass-through, and enterprise multi-model policies that cannot be ripped out.

### 10. Trade, tariff and customs compliance automation

**Why now.** Tariff policy has churned repeatedly. The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026; about **$100B of roughly $150B collected has been refunded**; Section 122 tariffs expired July 24, and Section 301 tariffs followed ([CRFB](https://www.crfb.org/blogs/12-month-rolling-deficit-19-trillion-july-2026); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/10/how-big-deficit-national-debt-tariffs-cbo/)). FEOC sourcing tests now reach virtually all energy credits ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-impact-on-the-iras-clean-energy-tax-credits.html)), turning bill-of-materials provenance into money.

**The 20x path.** The target is an AI-native platform that handles classification, duty optimization, refund recovery, country-of-origin and FEOC proofs for mid-market importers. **Inference:** this is the closest analog to Deel's "global compliance as a product" thesis. A $10B outcome is plausible if it becomes the system of record for cross-border goods, which means entry at or below $350M.

**Why Razavi.** This is the prepared-mind, cross-border, compliance-heavy pattern she reportedly used on Deel, after 18 months of tracking cross-border payroll (secondary source: [Antoine Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-yasmin-razavi/)).

**Risks and kill criteria.** Policy stabilization could shrink urgency, and large logistics incumbents could bundle. Kill a company whose revenue is one-off refund recoveries rather than recurring compliance.

**Signals.** Recurring per-shipment revenue, duty savings per customer, and expansion from tariffs into broader supply-chain provenance.

### 11. Climate-risk insurance underwriting and MGAs

**Why now.** Insured losses from the January 2025 LA fires were about **$40B**. California FAIR Plan enrollment rose **43%** between September 2024 and December 2025, and State Farm took a 17% emergency rate increase (single aggregator source: [CoverageCat](https://www.coveragecat.com/insurance-types/home/californias-home-insurance-crisis-whats-happening)). The retreat of traditional carriers is structural.

**The 20x path.** The target is an AI-underwritten MGA or capacity platform that prices mitigation (home hardening) and uses parametric products, backed by reinsurance and capital markets. **Inference:** insurance outcomes compound slowly, so 20x at growth needs entry at or below $300M and a path to a carrier-scale book. This is better suited to Spark's earlier capital or to a Series B.

**Why Razavi.** Capitolis and Marqeta give her capital-markets and regulated-fintech fluency, and this is a deeply unsexy workflow.

**Risks and kill criteria.** Catastrophe losses in the first books, reinsurance pricing, and state regulatory caps. Kill a company whose loss ratios exceed its model's predictions for two consecutive years.

**Signals.** Loss ratio versus benchmark, reinsurance capacity commitments, and verified mitigation data that lowers loss.

### 12. Robotics data layer and vertical robotics with unit economics

**Why now.** The humanoid and robot-foundation-model layer is closed at growth prices: Figure at about $39B, Skild at about $14B, Physical Intelligence at $5.6B ([HumanoidIndex](https://humanoidindex.org/funding); [Humanoid Intel](https://humanoidintel.ai/humanoid-robot-venture-capital/); [The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=6214)). Unitree's G1 lists at about $16K versus more than $200K for Western humanoids ([ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/humanoid-robots-in-2026-figure-apptronik-1x-and-tesla-optimus-compared)), so Western hardware cannot win on cost. The demographic pull is real: the US total fertility rate hit about **1.57** in 2025 ([WSJ via To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/why-the-u-s-fertility-rate-has-hit-a-record-low/amp/)), and the births-over-deaths surplus ends within a decade ([HR Brew](https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/2026/04/30/employers-should-expect-future-labor-headaches-as-us-birth-rate-falls-again)).

**The 20x path.** The targets are vertical robots for warehouse, food, inspection, lab automation or precision manufacturing that sell measurable labor replacement, and the data layer (teleoperation, data collection, evaluation) that every foundation-model company must buy. **Inference:** a vertical leader at $1B+ revenue is a $10B+ outcome, but few will get there by 2032, so this is a Series B bet, not a late-growth one.

**Why Razavi.** Highly technical founders and deep-tech capital intensity fit her MatX experience, but robotics is a new area for her, so conviction is lower.

**Risks and kill criteria.** Foundation-model companies building their own data operations, and hardware margins. Kill a company that cannot show payback under 24 months for its customers.

**Signals.** Robot uptime, fleet size under paid contract, customer payback period, and data revenue from multiple model developers.

### 13. Magnet recycling and rare-earth-free motors

**Why now.** China still licenses all exports of seven medium and heavy rare earths. Suspension of its broader October 2025 controls lapses **around November 10, 2026** ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later); [Materials Dispatch](https://www.materialsdispatch.com/en/countries/china)). The NdPr benchmark reportedly rose to about **$133/kg**, above the DoD's $110 floor for MP Materials (single market note: [Informed Clearly](https://informedclearly.com/en/geopolitics/49484/china-rare-earth-export-controls-2026); floor terms per [Columbia CGEP](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/mp-materials-deal-marks-a-significant-shift-in-us-rare-earths-policy/)).

**The 20x path.** Process technology and motor designs that remove heavy rare earths, sold to defense, EV and robotics OEMs. **Inference:** an outcome above $10B requires becoming a default supplier across several industries. The policy-driven price floor is a strong de-risker but also a ceiling on venture-style upside.

**Why Razavi.** Fit is moderate. This is capital-heavy industrial work, like Crusoe, but outside her fintech and AI core.

**Risks and kill criteria.** A US–China deal extending the truce, Chinese price dumping, and project-finance dependence. Kill a company whose unit economics require the price floor to hold forever.

**Signals.** Offtake contracts with defense primes, qualified material in production motors, and government equity or Office of Strategic Capital co-funding.

### 14. Event-contract infrastructure around prediction markets

**Why now.** Kalshi raised **$1B at $22B** in May 2026 and did about **$37.7B** of notional volume in July ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/kalshi/); [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/polymarket-eyes-15b-valuation-gap-with-kalshi-widens/)). Polymarket is reportedly raising near $15B after a $600M ICE investment.

**The 20x path and fit.** The top layer has consolidated. New bets are limited to tooling (data, market-making, compliance, "event contracts as a feature" for brokers and media). Kalshi is a Razavi key investment, so competing investments are conflicted. **Inference:** her best expression is pro-rata in Kalshi rather than a new 20x bet. Kalshi at $22B would need to reach about $440B to 20x, so this is a compounding position, not a 20x entry.

**Risks.** About **80% of Kalshi's volume is sports** ([Medium/0xicaruss citing Pew](https://medium.com/@0xicaruss/polymarket-vs-kalshi-in-2026-where-the-money-is-actually-moving-and-which-one-you-should-trade-1ddfd7a461e0)), which invites state gaming regulation.

### 15. GLP-1 economy cost management for employers and payers

**Why now.** About **11%** of US adults currently take a GLP-1 for weight loss, up from 3% in 2024 ([DigitalFoodLab citing Gallup](https://digitalfoodlab.com/glp-1-in-three-charts-sticky-adoption-and-what-next/)). Cash prices fell to **$149–$350 a month** under MFN deals ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-announces-major-developments-in-bringing-most-favored-nation-pricing-to-american-patients/)), and Medicare's $50 Bridge runs through 2027 ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicare/what-to-know-about-the-balance-model-for-glp-1s-in-medicare-and-medicaid/)). The FDA's crackdown on compounding closed the 2023–25 telehealth arbitrage ([Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-warning-letters-hims-novo-nordisk-compounded-glp1/)).

**The 20x path and fit.** The opportunity is in employer and payer benefit management for GLP-1 spend: persistence, maintenance and off-ramping, with outcomes guarantees. **Inference:** the outcome ceiling is probably $5–10B, so 20x requires entry near $250–400M. Her fit comes through the employer GTM channel she knows from Deel, but consumer health is not her pattern. Conviction is low-medium.

**Risks.** Part D sponsors balked at CMS's BALANCE model ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicare/what-to-know-about-the-balance-model-for-glp-1s-in-medicare-and-medicaid/)), so payer budgets are fragile, and drug-price declines shrink the savings pool.

## Where not to play: categories already priced past 20x or being commoditized

Several categories should be avoided as new growth entries, even though they dominate headlines. **Frontier labs** are the obvious case: the arithmetic above shows new money cannot 20x at Anthropic's $965B or OpenAI's $852B ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/openai/)). **AI coding assistants** are next. Cursor was reportedly at $50B, with an unverified $60B SpaceX deal ([letsdatascience](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/cursor-2-billion-funding-round-50-billion-valuation); [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/cursor-60-billion-valuation-anysphere-ai-coding-2026/)), and Claude Code and Codex compete directly. **Generic copilots and horizontal agent platforms** are being absorbed by lab products such as Cowork. **Voice infrastructure** is "largely funded," and the action has moved to applications ([AgentVoice](https://agentvoice.com/blog/ai-voice-agents-funding-market-map/)).

In the physical economy, **humanoid OEMs and robot foundation models** are $5–39B capital races with $1B+ rounds. **Reactor developers, fusion, launch and full-stack AVs** are mega-round territory. **Orbital compute** already prices success: Starcloud reportedly raised its Series A at $1.1B ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/starcloud-170-million-series-a-space-data-center-2026/)).

In health, **horizontal ambient scribes** are now an EHR feature. **Compounded-GLP-1 telehealth** is closed. **"Lab panel plus app" consumer diagnostics** faces ChatGPT Health, which reached all US users on Jul 23, 2026 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/health-in-chatgpt/)). **Bio foundation models and AI-first therapeutics** compete with Chinese fast followers and billion-dollar labs.

In fintech, **stablecoin issuance** is a squeezed, yield-capped business. **Prediction-market exchanges** have consolidated. **Credit-heavy consumer fintech** is punished by public markets: Klarna fell from a $15.1B IPO valuation to about $5B by one unverified estimate ([Mobibank](https://www.mobibank.fi/neobank-valuations-2026/)).

There is also a structural warning. Seed rounds of $100M or more now take about **20% of seed dollars** ([FourWeekMBA/Crunchbase](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-crunchbase-q3-2026-159b-venture-27-billion-dollar-rounds-ai/)). Researcher-led AI companies therefore often price at Series B levels before product, which compresses the growth-stage return window. A growth investor's edge comes from entering where the mega-funds are not crowding, as in the defense "hollowed middle," compliance-heavy fintech and administrative healthcare.

## Five macro scenarios that would break the list

**An AI-capex pause** is the biggest correlated risk. AI capex is about **2.4% of US GDP**, above dot-com telecom levels ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/16/is-the-ai-infrastructure-build-out-a-bubble-heres/)). Capex is growing faster than revenue while hyperscaler free cash flow trends down ([Futuriom](https://www.futuriom.com/articles/news/ctp-is-hyperscaler-ai-spending-sustainable/2026/04)). PitchBook warns that a market this dependent on one theme faces a broad correction if AI disappoints ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/09/pitchbook-us-venture-funding-hits-412-7b-first-half-ai-deals-dominate/)). Theses 1, 8 and 9 would be hit first. Theses 2 and 4 could benefit from cheaper overbuilt compute, the telecom-2001 analog Benedict Evans invokes ([TechWolf](https://www.techwolf.ai/resources/blog/reading-the-ai-shift-our-cto-jeroen-van-hautte-meets-benedict-evans)).

**A sustained hiking cycle** is the second. The Fed reportedly hiked to **3.75–4.00%** on Sep 16, 2026, the first hike since 2023 (blog source, unverified against the Fed: [keepingupwithinflation](https://keepingupwithinflation.com/post/fomc-september-16-2026-rate-hike/)), and Barclays expects two more ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/barclays-sees-two-more-fed-rate-hikes-this-year-after-warsh-speech-472100)). Core CPI is about 3.1% ([Octagon](https://www.octagonai.co/markets/fomc/economic-policy/fed-decision-in-september.md)). Higher rates hurt long-duration bio (6), debt-dependent hardware (8, 13) and insurance float assumptions (11).

**The labs moving up the stack** is the third. The February 2026 "SaaSpocalypse" erased about $2T of software value ([tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/saas-stock-crash-ai-agents-2-trillion-2026/)). If labs ship credible vertical agents in finance operations or revenue cycle, theses 2 and 5 lose their moat. The test is whether companies hold liability, licenses and integrations that general agents cannot.

**A geopolitical shock** is the fourth. A Chinese rare-earth snapback after November 2026 would help 7 and 13 but raise costs for 8 and 12. A renewed Hormuz closure, which pushed Brent above $100 in March 2026 ([Oman Observer/AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1187013/world/region/what-is-the-global-economic-fallout-of-the-iran-war)), would worsen inflation and the rate path. Iranian strikes on Gulf infrastructure have already put a physical-risk discount on Gulf data-center demand.

**Regulatory reversal and a shut exit window** is the fifth. A defunded WISeR, slow GENIUS rulemaking, a CLARITY stall lasting into 2027, and state action on sports-heavy prediction markets would each hit specific theses. More broadly, if the IPO market keeps punishing non-infrastructure listings, growth-stage 20x outcomes outside AI infrastructure will depend on a small set of strategic acquirers.

## Conclusion

The cycle has shifted the edge from picking models to picking chokepoints. Razavi's Anthropic win came from underwriting a capability before the market priced it. The 2026 analog is underwriting the constraints that capability now runs into: megawatts, payment rails that agents and global businesses can trust, and the labor-heavy back offices that models can finally absorb. These categories share an awkward property for most growth investors. They require fluency in both frontier AI and regulated, GTM-heavy infrastructure, and that combination is unusually rare and closely matches her portfolio.

The list is also more correlated than it looks. Most top theses ride AI capex and agent capability, so a disciplined portfolio would pair the high-conviction AI-adjacent bets (1, 2, 4, 9) with theses that pay off in the opposite scenario. Trade compliance, climate insurance, healthcare administration and defense supply chain do well when rates stay high, tariffs churn and geopolitics worsen. Where the market is too young for a growth entry, most clearly agent trust infrastructure, the best move is for Spark's earlier capital to take the first position so she can later scale it with growth dollars.
